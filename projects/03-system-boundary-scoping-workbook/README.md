# 03 — System Boundary Scoping Workbook

**What it is.** A structured interview that defines what is actually in scope
for CUI before a single control gets assessed.

**Why it matters.** Scope is the highest-leverage decision in a CMMC
engagement. Every asset in scope must meet 110 requirements, be documented, and
be assessed. The most expensive mistake a small contractor makes is scoping too
wide — usually by default rather than by decision.

**Build time.** 4–6 hours.
**Sells as.** A $500–$1,500 standalone scoping session, and the thing that makes
your fixed-fee quotes accurate.

---

## Why this is the first thing you sell

Two commercial reasons:

1. **It prices the rest of the work.** You cannot quote a fixed fee for an
   assessment before you know the asset count. Sell the scoping session, then
   quote off its output. This alone will stop you losing money on fixed-fee work.
2. **It is a small yes.** A prospect who will not sign a $12,000 documentation
   engagement will sign a $750 scoping session. Do it well and the larger
   engagement is a formality.

---

## Step-by-step

### Step 1 — Learn the asset categories

From the CMMC scoping guidance:

| Category | Requirement |
|---|---|
| CUI asset | All 110; assessed |
| Security protection asset | All 110; assessed |
| Contractor risk managed asset | Documented; assessed only for the policy |
| Specialised asset | Documented with risk treatment; limited assessment |
| Out of scope | Not assessed — separation must be demonstrable |

The distinction between *contractor risk managed* and *out of scope* is where
most of the money is, and where most people get it wrong.

### Step 2 — Run the interview

`scoping-interview.md`. Five parts:

1. Does CUI exist here at all?
2. Where does it come from and go?
3. Asset classification
4. The reduction conversation
5. The boundary statement

**Part 2 has seven "shadow path" questions.** They find the flows people forget
— the personal Dropbox, the one-time personal email, the shop-floor PC with a
shared login. Ask all seven, every time. They are where the surprises live.

### Step 3 — Complete the asset inventory

`asset-inventory.csv`, one row per asset, every asset classified with a
justification. The justification column is not optional: an out-of-scope claim
you cannot defend becomes an in-scope asset the moment an assessor asks.

### Step 4 — Have the reduction conversation

Part 4 is where you earn the fee. For each option, quantify:

> "Consolidating CUI to a single enclave takes 34 assets out of scope. That is
> roughly six weeks of assessment and documentation you no longer pay for."

### Step 5 — Write the boundary statement

One paragraph that must survive being read aloud to an assessor. Template is in
Part 5. It goes straight into SSP Section 4.

---

## The advice that wins you clients

Question 1.5 asks whether the work could be done without receiving CUI at all.
Sometimes the answer is yes — the prime holds it, or work happens in the
customer's enclave.

**Tell them.** You lose that engagement and gain a reputation as the consultant
who does not sell work people do not need. In a referral-driven market that
trade is strongly in your favour, and small defense contractors talk to each
other constantly.
