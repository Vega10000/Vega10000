# Positioning

The single decision that determines whether marketing works. Get this wrong and
no amount of content, outreach, or SEO fixes it.

## The mistake almost everyone makes

"Cybersecurity consultant." Nobody searches for that, nobody refers it, and it
competes with every firm on earth.

## The position

> **CMMC Level 2 readiness for small defense contractors — the 10-to-100-person
> machine shops, engineering firms, and suppliers who have been told by a prime
> that they need this and have no idea where to start.**

Narrow on three axes at once:

| Axis | Choice | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Who** | Small DoD suppliers, 10–100 staff | Underserved. Big firms chase primes. |
| **What** | CMMC L2 / NIST 800-171 only | A specialist beats a generalist at 3x the rate |
| **When** | Pre-assessment readiness | A deadline exists, so budget exists |

## Why this specific niche works

1. **A regulation creates the demand.** They are not buying because security is
   a good idea. They are buying because a contract requires it.
2. **There is a deadline.** Deadlines convert. "Should do" never does.
3. **The buyer is reachable.** SAM.gov lists them. Primes publish supplier
   lists. They cluster geographically around bases and industrial areas.
4. **Big firms ignore them.** A $40k engagement is too small for a large
   consultancy and exactly right for one person.
5. **They talk to each other.** Small defense suppliers in a region all know
   each other. Referrals compound fast.

## The one-sentence version

> "I help small defense contractors get CMMC-ready without paying enterprise
> consulting rates for it."

Use it in your LinkedIn headline, your email signature, and the first sentence
of every call.

## What you are selling

Not documents. Not compliance. **Removing the risk of losing a contract.**

The SSP is the deliverable. The purchase is the certainty that their prime will
not drop them. Price and pitch against that, not against page count.

## Messages that land, and their weaker versions

| Say | Not |
|---|---|
| "Most companies scope too wide and pay for it twice over" | "I do gap assessments" |
| "You need a defensible boundary before you assess anything" | "I follow NIST 800-171" |
| "An SSP claiming controls you don't have is worse than an honest gap" | "I write compliance documentation" |
| "I'll tell you if you don't need this" | "I offer free consultations" |

The left column demonstrates expertise. The right column describes a commodity.

## The claim you must never make

You cannot certify anyone. Only an authorised C3PAO can. Every piece of
marketing must be clear that you deliver **readiness and documentation**.

Say "CMMC readiness", "assessment preparation", "800-171 documentation".
Never "CMMC certification" as something you provide. Getting loose with this is
both a credibility problem and a complaint you cannot defend.

## Credibility on day one, before you have clients

- The home lab and its worked SSP (project 07)
- A public teardown of a real 800-171 requirement people get wrong
- Security+ (held) and CCP (in progress) stated plainly
- Free work for one company in exchange for a testimonial and a case study

Nobody expects a new consultant to have twenty clients. They expect evidence
that you know the material. The lab plus one detailed public writeup is enough
to start.
