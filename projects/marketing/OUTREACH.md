# Outreach

Marketing that works before you have an audience. Content compounds but is
slow; outreach is immediate. Do both, weight toward outreach in month one.

## Finding the buyers

| Source | How |
|---|---|
| **SAM.gov** | Filter active registrations by NAICS (332, 336, 541330, 541712) and employee count. Public and free. |
| **Prime supplier lists** | Many publish supplier diversity or small-business lists. |
| **DIB associations** | NDIA, state manufacturing extension partnerships, regional defense alliances. |
| **USAspending.gov** | Contract awards by size and location. A recent award is a company with money and an obligation. |
| **LinkedIn Sales Navigator** | Filter: industry defense/aerospace, 10–200 employees, your region. |

Start local. A 45-minute drive is a meaningful advantage with this buyer — they
prefer someone who will show up.

## Who to contact

In a 10–100 person defense supplier, the buyer is almost never a CISO. Look
for: **Owner / President**, **VP Operations**, **Quality Manager** (often
already owns ISO/AS9100 and is handed CMMC by default), or **Contracts
Manager** (the one who reads DFARS clauses).

The Quality Manager is the most underrated. They already own an audit regime
and are usually the person quietly panicking.

## Cold email

Short, specific, one ask. Under 120 words.

> **Subject:** CMMC question for [Company]
>
> Hi [Name],
>
> I work with small defense suppliers on CMMC Level 2 readiness — mostly
> machine shops and engineering firms in [REGION] who've been told by a prime
> they need it and aren't sure where to start.
>
> The thing that costs companies the most isn't the controls, it's scope. Most
> assess their whole network when only part of it ever touches CUI, and pay
> two or three times what they needed to.
>
> I put together a short worksheet on scoping — 12 questions, no email required:
> [LINK]
>
> If it's useful and you want to talk through your situation, I'm happy to.
> If CMMC isn't on your radar yet, ignore this entirely.
>
> [NAME]

Why it works: names their world in the first line, leads with a specific
insight, gives before asking, and gives explicit permission to say no.

## Follow-up

Two follow-ups, then stop.

**+5 days:**
> Hi [Name] — following up on the scoping worksheet. The question most people
> get wrong is #7, whether an asset that *could* reach CUI but isn't meant to
> is in scope. It usually is, unless you can demonstrate separation. Happy to
> walk through it. [NAME]

**+12 days:**
> Hi [Name] — last one from me. If CMMC becomes a priority, the worksheet is
> here: [LINK]. Good luck with it either way. [NAME]

Then stop. Three touches is persistence; five is harassment, and this is a
community that talks.

## LinkedIn

The platform where this buyer actually is.

**Profile.** Headline: *"CMMC Level 2 readiness for small defense contractors |
NIST 800-171 | SSP, POA&M, gap assessments"*. Searchable terms, not "Cybersecurity
Professional". Featured section: the lead magnet.

**Posting.** Twice a week, one specific thing you know:
- One requirement people misread, and what it actually demands
- A scoping decision and what it saved
- A finding from your lab and how you documented around it
- A plain-language explainer of something the DFARS clause requires

**Connecting.** 15–20 targeted requests a week with a note. Do not pitch in the
request. Do not pitch in the first reply either.

**Commenting.** Ten minutes daily on posts by primes, DIB associations, and
CMMC commentators. Substantive comments on other people's posts reach more of
the right people than your own posts do, early on.

## The referral engine

Referrals are the highest-converting channel and the one people forget to build.

**Ask at handover, when satisfaction peaks:**
> "Glad this landed well. Most of my work comes through referral — is there
> anyone else in your supply chain facing the same deadline who'd find this
> useful?"

**Partner with adjacent providers.** Managed service providers, ISO/AS9100
consultants, and defense-focused accountants all meet your buyer and do not
compete with you. A standing referral relationship with two MSPs can fill a
pipeline on its own.

**Stay in touch with everyone you decline.** The prospect with no contract
requirement today has one in eighteen months, and they remember the person who
told them not to buy.
