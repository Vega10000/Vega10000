# Product Ladder

Five rungs, from a free download to a retained relationship. Each rung is a
larger commitment and each one makes the next easier to sell.

| # | Offer | Price | Delivery | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Scoping worksheet | Free | Instant | Capture the email, prove competence |
| 1 | Documentation kit | $99–$299 | Instant | First purchase, funds nothing but builds trust |
| 2 | Scoping session | $500–$1,500 | Half a day | First *service* purchase, prices everything else |
| 3 | Gap assessment | $2,500–$6,000 | 2–3 weeks | The core engagement |
| 4 | Documentation build | $6,000–$18,000 | 4–8 weeks | The large engagement |
| 5 | Readiness retainer | $2,000–$5,000/mo | Ongoing | Recurring revenue |

## Why the order matters

Nobody hires a stranger for $18,000. They will spend $99 to see whether you
know your material, and $750 to have you look at their environment. Each rung
de-risks the next.

The rung people skip is **2**. It is the most important one you have: it is a
small, easy yes that makes your large quotes accurate, and a client who has
paid you once is a fundamentally different prospect from one who has not.

## Realistic revenue, and the honest version

**Products alone are not a business.** At $149, twenty sales is $3,000. That is
a good month of side income, not a living. Products exist to build trust and
capture email addresses; services are where the money is. Anyone telling you a
template kit scales to six figures is selling you a course about selling
courses.

A realistic first-year mix for one person working evenings and weekends:

| Source | Year 1 |
|---|---|
| Product sales | $2,000–$6,000 |
| Scoping sessions (10–15) | $8,000–$15,000 |
| Gap assessments (4–6) | $12,000–$30,000 |
| One documentation build | $8,000–$15,000 |
| **Total side income** | **$30,000–$65,000** |

On top of a salary, that is a meaningful year. It is not $200k, and it takes
most of your evenings. Anyone promising faster is describing an outcome they
have not had.

## The constraint nobody mentions

Services do not scale with one person. At $150/hour and 15 billable hours a
week alongside a full-time job, the ceiling is roughly $115k/year of service
revenue — and you will not sustain 15 hours a week indefinitely.

Getting past that ceiling requires one of:

- **Raise rates.** The fastest lever, and the one people avoid. Specialists with
  case studies charge $200–$300/hour. Same work, different price.
- **Productise a repeatable piece.** A fixed-scope, fixed-price offering you
  have delivered enough times to do in half the hours you charge for.
- **Subcontract delivery.** Hire someone to do the documentation while you scope
  and sell. This is a real business with real management overhead.
- **Stop selling hours.** Retainers and fixed fees decouple price from time.

Pick one deliberately rather than drifting.

## Pricing principles

1. **Price the outcome, not the hours.** A company with a $4M contract at risk
   is buying certainty, not 80 hours.
2. **Never be the cheapest.** In compliance, cheap signals inexperience. The
   client who picks you on price will dispute the invoice.
3. **Always take a deposit.** 40% before work starts, no exceptions.
   Non-payment is the most common way small consultancies get hurt.
4. **Raise prices every third engagement** until you start losing deals. You are
   underpriced until roughly one in four prospects says no on price.
5. **Charge for scoping.** Free scoping trains clients to extract your most
   valuable thinking for nothing.
