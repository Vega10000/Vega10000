# Legal and Risk

The part that gets skipped and is the part that ends businesses. Read before
you take a dollar.

> Not legal advice. Talk to an attorney and an accountant in your state before
> trading. This is a list of what to ask them about.

## 1. Form an entity before your first client

Operating as a sole proprietor means your personal assets are exposed to any
claim from your consulting work. An LLC is inexpensive in most states and takes
a day.

Ask an attorney about: entity type for your state and situation, operating
agreement, and keeping business and personal finances genuinely separate — a
commingled LLC provides much less protection than people assume.

## 2. Get professional liability insurance

Errors and omissions cover, before your first engagement. The scenario it
exists for: you assess a client, they submit a score, an assessor disagrees, and
they lose a contract and look for someone to blame.

For a solo consultant expect roughly $600–$1,500/year for $1M cover. Some
clients will require proof before signing.

Cyber liability is worth considering separately — you will hold client
documentation describing their security weaknesses, which is sensitive material
in its own right.

## 3. Contract before every engagement

Use the generator on your profile page as a starting point, then **have an
attorney review it once**. A single review of a template you reuse is a few
hundred dollars and covers every future engagement.

Non-negotiable clauses:

- **No guarantee of certification outcome.** You provide readiness and
  documentation. Certification is decided by a C3PAO on facts you do not
  control.
- **Limitation of liability**, capped at fees paid.
- **Client responsibility for accuracy.** You document what they tell you and
  what you observe. If they misrepresent their environment, that is on them —
  say so in writing.
- **Payment terms with a deposit.** 40% up front.
- **IP ownership.** They own the deliverables; you keep your templates and
  methods. Without this you cannot reuse your own work.

## 4. The claims you must not make

| Never say | Say instead |
|---|---|
| "I'll certify you" | "I'll get you assessment-ready" |
| "Guaranteed to pass" | "This addresses the requirements an assessor evaluates" |
| "CMMC certified consultant" | "CMMC Certified Professional (CCP)" — only once you hold it |
| "Government approved" | Nothing. There is no such endorsement. |

The CMMC ecosystem has defined roles — C3PAO, CCP, CCA — administered by the
Cyber AB. Claiming or implying a credential you do not hold is a complaint to
that body, and it is the kind of thing competitors report.

## 5. Handling client data

You will hold documents describing exactly where a defense contractor is weak.
Treat that as seriously as you would want your own vendor to.

- Encrypt at rest and in transit
- MFA on everything
- Do not keep client data after an engagement ends beyond what the contract
  requires; return or destroy it and say so in writing
- If a client's data is export-controlled (ITAR/EAR), **that has its own legal
  regime** and can restrict who may access it, including by nationality. Ask
  about ITAR on intake and get advice before accepting that work.

## 6. The conflict most people miss

**Check your employment contract before you take a single client.** Many have
outside-work, non-compete, or IP-assignment clauses. An IP-assignment clause can
mean work you produce on your own time belongs to your employer — which would
include the kit in this folder.

Read it. If it is ambiguous, ask HR in writing. Discovering this after you have
clients is a genuinely bad position, and it is the most common way a side
consulting business ends badly.

## 7. Tax

- Self-employment tax applies to consulting income and surprises people badly
- Set aside 25–30% of every payment from the first one
- Quarterly estimated payments are likely required
- Talk to an accountant in month one, not at year end

## 8. Refunds

Publish a refund policy on any product you sell. A simple, generous one (14
days, no questions) reduces disputes, which cost more than refunds do.

Digital products have high chargeback rates. A clear policy, an actual delivery
email, and a real support address are your defence.
