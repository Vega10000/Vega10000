# Client Intake Questionnaire

Ten minutes on the first call, before price comes up. Every answer maps to a
scope multiplier in `scope_calculator.py`.

## A — The obligation

| # | Question | Answer | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | Which contract requires CMMC or DFARS 7012? | | No contract, no engagement |
| A2 | Who is the prime, or is this direct to DoD? | | Primes often dictate the timeline |
| A3 | What level are you being asked for? | | L1 self-assessment is a different, much smaller job |
| A4 | Is there an assessment date? | | Drives the rush multiplier |
| A5 | Has anyone told you in writing what is CUI? | | If not, that is step one |

## E — Commercial

| # | Question | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| E1 | What happens to the business if this is not done in time? | |
| E2 | What is the contract worth annually? | |
| E3 | Have you budgeted for this? What range? | |
| E4 | Are you talking to other consultants? | |

> **E1 and E2 set your price ceiling.** A company with a $4M contract at risk
> values a $15k engagement very differently from one exploring their options.
> This is not exploitation — it is pricing to the value delivered, which is the
> only sustainable basis for professional services.

---

## Scoring the call

**Green — quote it:**
contract requires it · realistic date · named internal owner · budget exists ·
they can describe their environment

**Amber — proceed carefully:**
date is tight but movable · owner exists but is stretched · budget is vague ·
environment is messier than they think

**Red — decline:**
no contract requires it · impossible fixed date · nobody internal will own it ·
they want certification from you rather than readiness · cheapest-option shopping

---

## What you say at the end of the call

> "That's everything I need. Here's what I'd suggest: we start with a scoping
> session, which is [PRICE] and takes about half a day. That gives us a defined
> boundary and an accurate asset count, and I'll quote the rest of the work off
> that. I'd rather price it properly than guess and be wrong in either
> direction. I'll send a proposal within two business days."

This does three things: it sells a small, easy yes; it makes the larger quote
accurate; and it signals you scope rather than guess. Consultants who quote
immediately signal the opposite.
