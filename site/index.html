02

Where CMMC actually stands

as of 5 Sep 2026

On 13 July 2026 the Department suspended CMMC Phase 2 and launched a review of the whole programme. A lot of contractors read the headline and concluded the requirement went away. It did not — only the assessment mechanism did. The Task Force reports within days of this writing, so expect this picture to move.

Suspended · 13 Jul 2026

What was paused

  • ✕Level 2 third-party assessments. C3PAO certification is not required while the suspension holds.
  • ✕Level 3 DIBCAC assessments. Same suspension.
  • ✕The 10 Nov 2026 deadline. Phase 2 milestones are halted, not merely deferred to a new date.
  • ✕Contract designations. Affected solicitations and contracts are to be amended to remove the suspended requirements.
Unchanged · still binding

What you still owe

  • →DFARS 252.204-7012. Untouched. If it is in your contract, it still binds you.
  • →NIST SP 800-171 Rev 2. All 110 requirements. Rev 3 is published but Rev 2 remains the operative baseline.
  • →A current, accurate SPRS score. The self-assessment was never part of Phase 2 and was not suspended.
  • →72-hour incident reporting to DIBNET, and the cloud security requirements.
  • →DIBCAC spot-checks. They continue against self-reported scores, independent of the pause.
In motion

What happens next

  • ·CMMC Reform Task Force, reporting to the CIO, reviewing cost drivers and control effectiveness.
  • ·Industry RFI closed 14 Aug 2026 — readiness, cost, and which controls actually produce outcomes.
  • ·Recommendations due on or about 13 Sep 2026. Not published as at the date above — days away, so treat this page as provisional.
  • ·Plan for change, not repeal. Nothing announced suggests CUI protection stops being required.
Verified 5 Sep 2026 against public reporting and law-firm analysis of the 13 July memoranda. Re-verify before every client conversation. DFARS 7012 Federal Register

The practical read. The certification mechanism is on hold. The legal obligation to protect Controlled Unclassified Information is not, and neither is the requirement to tell the government a true score. An inaccurate SPRS submission carries False Claims Act exposure whether or not an assessor is scheduled to visit — that risk did not move on 13 July.

Companies that stop now will restart under time pressure when the Task Force reports. Companies that keep going hold an accurate score, a real SSP, and a working POA&M whatever the programme is called next. If your deadline was the only reason you were doing this, that is worth an honest conversation — and I will have it with you.
